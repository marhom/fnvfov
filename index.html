Motion Sickness and FOV

TLDR:

Low fields of view in games can exacerbate (or wholly cause) motion sickness.

To see if this helps: change your FOV to 90+ in most games.

In FNV:
1) Console (disables achievements unless certain mods are installed, can be used to test) fov # (ex: fov 95 )
2) .ini - FNV can get weird and finicky about the .ini getting reset to default, but here are the two major commands for posterity
- fDefaultWorldFOV - This is the game FOV and the one you primarily want to change. Defaults to 75, set to 95+. This is probably not in the ini file by default, add it under [Display]
- fDefaultFOV - FOV of menus. leave this alone
- fPipboy1stPersonFOV - pipboy FOV. This is probably fine at default of 47
- fDefault1stPersonFOV - these are your arms. I changed this to be 75 @ 95 world FOV
in Fallout.ini / FalloutPrefs.ini / Fallout_default.ini / Fallout_custom.ini:
fPipboy1stPersonFOV=47.0
fDefault1stPersonFOV=75.0000
fDefaultWorldFOV=95.0000
3) mod - FOV Slider is the first one I slammed in to fuck around with; it was really easy to get up and running - it requires NVSE + the JIP LN NVSE Plugin and the Mod Configuration Menu and is probably the way I'd approach this. It adds a very simple menu to your esc menu that lets you tweak all the options (including some of the more esoteric scope related ones) on the fly.

Motion Sickness

This isn't intended to be a silver bullet (or imply it is one) - stuff's complex, and there's lots of reasons that moving things - interactive and not - can make someone sick. That said, FOV changes can be a pretty serious scalar - fucking with it might not solve the problem but it might improve the average case a hell of a lot.

As a personal testimonial: I played Fallout 3 at launch - which has the same FOV problems as FNV - and never finished. Partially, this was due to my adhd; the other part was that it fucking sucked to play and I didn't understand what my body was telling me. I couldn't play in first person at all, and third person would routinely still make the game too uncomfortable to play indoors. I didn't know what the fuck at the time - I had some other similar problems with things like Half-Life 2, but never connected them. It wasn't until a few years later when I got mad at never having finished HL2 that I went and tried to figure out why I went from "not feeling great" to "I want to die and also have upended my entire digestive system at my monitor" within two seconds of getting into a vehicle. I hit on some stuff about FOV and started looking into that and, well: now I can basically play anything that has an FOV slider / access to cvars / if I can set up the physical space (distance, etc) right. Since tweaking my FOV in my recovered Fallout 3 save i have played for hours with zero complaints other than "god some of this fucking sucks ass for reasons unrelated to nausea".

Field of View (FOV):

just going to cite wikipedia here

In video games the field of view or field of vision (abbreviated FOV) is the extent of the observable game world that is seen on the display at any given moment. It is typically measured as an angle, although whether this angle is the horizontal, vertical, or diagonal component of the field of view varies from game to game.

- Field of View in Video Games


When we talk about "FOV", the number in question is that interior camera angle of the green area. 90 - 1/4th of 360 degrees, so effectively one "direction" of vision - is a pretty good standard for comfort, but it may change some of the aesthetic decisions made in game. For instance, I mentioned HL2 before - there's FOV changes there that (IIRC? maybe it's in the PCG article) were made to bring the camera in tighter to showcase the expressiveness of the facial animations in Source; increasing the FOV to a playable number zooms the camera back out more.

Practically, what this means is that if you increase the FOV, you increase the visible area of the game world that you can see. This gives a more immersive feel - you're not looking through a plate of glass, you're seeing things move in your periphery somewhat correctly, etc.
